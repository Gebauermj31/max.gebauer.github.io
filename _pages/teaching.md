## My approach

I build courses around active reasoning. In philosophy, that means reconstructing arguments precisely and testing them against scientific practice. In statistics, it means using real problems to connect computation, interpretation, and uncertainty. Across both, I make the structure of expert thinking visible: how to define a question, choose evidence, diagnose a model, and revise a claim.

I have taught at the University of Pennsylvania in instructor and teaching-assistant roles since 2023, across Philosophy, Statistics & Data Science, and Health Policy.

## Courses taught

### Philosophy of Science

As instructor, I guide students through explanation, confirmation, causation, models, values in science, and scientific change. The course treats philosophy as a practical way to understand how evidence supports claims—not as a set of views to memorize.

### Introduction to Statistics

As instructor, I introduce statistical reasoning through data, emphasizing variation, study design, estimation, uncertainty, and the interpretation of models. Students learn to connect calculations to substantive questions and to recognize when an apparently precise answer rests on fragile assumptions.
