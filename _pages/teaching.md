## My approach

Teaching must remain responsive to its educational context: different groups of students bring different needs, and emerging tools such as generative AI change both what students can do and what genuine understanding looks like. I treat course design as an ongoing process of observing where students struggle, revising the route into difficult material, and creating assessments that reveal reasoning rather than reward surface familiarity.

<div class="teaching-principles wide">
  <section class="teaching-principle">
    <span class="meta-label">Transfer</span>
    <h3>Explain it beyond the classroom</h3>
    <p>A strong sign of understanding is the ability to teach an idea, at an appropriate level, to someone who did not take the course. That conviction motivates my use of oral examinations in philosophy and my emphasis on interpretation and explanation in statistics.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="teaching-principle">
    <span class="meta-label">Judgment</span>
    <h3>Go beyond formulas</h3>
    <p>Students should know why a method works, which questions it can answer, when its assumptions are plausible, and how to move from a question posed in ordinary language to a complete analytic strategy.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="teaching-principle">
    <span class="meta-label">Exchange</span>
    <h3>Let each field teach the other</h3>
    <p>Statistics teaching helps me identify sticking points when philosophy becomes formal. Philosophy teaching helps me make the conceptual frameworks and sometimes-mysterious assumptions behind statistical practice more visible.</p>
  </section>
</div>

I have taught learners from high school through graduate study. At the University of Pennsylvania, I have held instructor and teaching-assistant roles since 2023 across Philosophy, Statistics and Data Science, and Health Policy Research; I have also taught in Wharton summer programs for high school students.
